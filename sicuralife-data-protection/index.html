Module 1

What Personal Data We Handle — and Why It's Sensitive

"Personal data" isn't just names in a spreadsheet. At Sicuralife, it includes information tied to a real emergency involving a real, often elderly, person.

📍
What we hold: end-customer name and address (via our B2B partners), device identifiers (IMEI, ICCID, Watch Code), GPS location at the moment of an SOS alert, call and support records in Odoo — and, as vital-sign features are developed (opportunity 02), potentially heart rate or oxygen data too.
📇
"Ordinary" personal data

Name, address, phone number, device serial numbers — handled through partners and Odoo.

❤️
Potentially sensitive data

Real-time location during a fall, and future vital-sign readings — this can edge into "special category" health data under GDPR Article 9, which needs extra care.

Quick check: myth or fact?

"We're a hardware company, so data protection rules mostly don't apply to us."
Myth. We process location, contact, and device data for real people every day — that's exactly what these rules cover.
"A GPS location captured during a fall alert counts as personal data."
Fact. Any data that can identify or locate a specific person is personal data.
"Future vital-sign data (heart rate, oxygen) would need extra care compared to a device serial number."
Fact. Health-related data is a "special category" under GDPR, with stricter rules than ordinary device data.

Quick scenario check

SituationA partner asks you to send over "just the GPS logs" for a batch of devices to help debug a connectivity issue.
GPS logs tied to specific end customers are personal data — "just technical" doesn't make that go away.
Exactly — minimise what's shared and confirm it's actually needed before sending anything.

GDPR & Swiss FADP — the Basics That Apply to Us

Sicuralife is a Swiss company, but our partners and end customers are in Switzerland, France, the UK, and Italy. That means two data protection laws apply at the same time, not just one.

EU GDPRSwiss FADP (revised, since 01.09.2023)
Applies to us becauseWe offer a service to people in the EU/UK (France, UK joint venture, Italy)We're a Swiss company processing personal data
AuthorityNational data protection authorities in each EU countryFederal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC)
Breach notificationWithin 72 hours to the authority if there's a risk to people"As soon as possible" to the FDPIC if there's a high risk
Special category / sensitive dataHealth data needs extra legal grounds (Art. 9)Health data is also specially protected under Swiss law
⚖️
Both apply, not "either/or": a French end customer's data is protected by GDPR; a Swiss end customer's data is protected by the FADP. Since we serve both, we design our processes to meet the stricter of the two wherever they differ. This is tracked as risk 08 in our QF14 Risk Register ("Regulatory Noncompliance").

Quick scenario check

SituationYou hear a colleague say "GDPR doesn't apply to us, we're Swiss, not an EU company."
GDPR reaches beyond EU borders whenever you offer services to people who live there — being Swiss doesn't exempt us.
Exactly — both laws apply, depending on where the end customer is.

Module 3

Data Subject Rights & Handling Requests

Anyone whose data we hold — an end customer, a partner contact — has rights over that data. If a request comes in, how we handle it matters just as much as the answer itself.

Access

Someone can ask what data we hold about them and why.

Correction

They can ask us to fix inaccurate data.

Deletion

They can ask us to erase their data, where there's no legal reason to keep it.

Objection

They can object to certain uses of their data.

👤
Who handles this at Sicuralife: right now, that's Jérôme, who combines the Quality Representative and management roles (see risk register item 16). If you receive this kind of request directly — from a partner or end customer — don't try to resolve it yourself: forward it to Jérôme straight away so it's handled correctly and on time.

If something goes wrong: breach notification

⏱️
GDPR: 72 hours

A personal data breach with risk to people must be reported to the relevant EU authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of it.

⏱️
Swiss FADP: "as soon as possible"

A high-risk breach must be reported to the FDPIC without unreasonable delay.

Right — pass it on immediately rather than acting on it, or sitting on it.

Module 4

Keeping Data Safe in Daily Work

Most data protection isn't dramatic — it's the everyday habit of not sharing more than needed, and keeping data where it belongs.

Odoo ERP & Sicuralife Cloud PlatformWhere customer, device and support data actually lives (Swiss hosting, Kubernetes cluster).
Systems
↓only what's needed, only to those who need it
Call-centre & integration partnersSkyresponse, Ennovation and similar partners only receive what's required to route an emergency alert.
Processors
↓
Nothing extra leaves the systemNo personal spreadsheets, no ad-hoc exports "just to check something."
Safe

Do

  • Keep customer and device data inside Odoo / the Sicuralife Cloud Platform
  • Share only the minimum data needed with a partner, for a specific purpose
  • Use strong, unique passwords and keep your account access to yourself

Don't

  • Export customer lists or GPS logs to a personal laptop, USB stick, or private email
  • Send full customer data to a partner "to be safe" when a subset would do
  • Share your login credentials with anyone, even a colleague
🔒
This connects directly to risk 04 in our QF14 Risk Register (cybersecurity / unauthorised access) and the planned ISO 27001 assessment — good data-handling habits are the first line of defence, before any technical control kicks in.

Exactly — never real personal data for something like a demo, however convincing it would look.

Five Rules to Remember

  1. Location, contact, and device data are personal data — and future vital-sign data would be especially sensitive.
  2. Both GDPR and the Swiss FADP apply to us at the same time, depending on where the end customer is.
  3. Never resolve a data subject request or a suspected breach yourself — forward it to Jérôme immediately.
  4. Share only the minimum data needed, and only through Odoo / the Cloud Platform — never personal copies or exports.
  5. Real customer data never appears in a demo, a press example, or anywhere it isn't strictly needed.
