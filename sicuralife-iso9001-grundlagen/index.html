Module 1

What Is Quality — and How Do We Keep Improving It?

Before we look at any document or form: what does "quality" actually mean under ISO 9001 — and how does Sicuralife keep improving it, batch after batch?

📐
ISO 9000 definition: quality is the degree to which a set of inherent characteristics of a product fulfils requirements. Not "more luxury." Meeting the requirement — every time.

Quality has nothing to do with price

✔ Quality A budget airline that gets every passenger there safely and on time. It meets exactly what it promised.
✘ Not quality A five-star hotel that hands you a room that wasn't cleaned. Price says luxury — the outcome fails its own requirement.
⌚
What "the requirement" means for us

Our watch has to detect a fall and trigger an SOS call — every time, for every unit that ships. That's the requirement. Quality is how reliably we meet it.

Quick check: myth or fact?

"Quality means premium materials and a higher price."
Myth. ISO 9001 quality means meeting the requirement — not price or luxury.
"A cheaper product can still be high quality."
Fact. As long as it reliably meets what it's supposed to do.
"For a safety device like our watch, 'quality' can directly affect whether an SOS call goes through."
Fact. Exactly why quality isn't paperwork for us — it's how we work.
"Quality is mostly the Quality Manager's job."
Myth. Production oversight, pre-shipment checks, support calls, sales promises — everyone's daily work feeds into it.

How we keep improving: the PDCA cycle

ISO 9001's continuous-improvement engine is the PDCA cycle (Plan – Do – Check – Act). It isn't a one-off project — it's a loop that starts again every time.

Plan

Notice a recurring issue, set a goal, decide what "better" looks like.

At SicuralifeSIM insertion and cloud configuration still happen at Jérôme's home instead of the Contone warehouse — so we plan to move the step there, with a real workstation.
Do

Carry out the change — on a small scale first if possible.

At SicuralifeSet up the workstation in Contone and run the next batch's SIM insertion and configuration there instead of at home.
Check

Measure: did it actually get better? Compare against the plan.

At SicuralifeCheck that the batch shipped on time with the correct SIM and configuration — with real evidence, not just a one-off demo for the audit.
Act

Standardize what worked, adjust what didn't — then the loop starts again.

At SicuralifeIf it worked: Contone becomes the standard process going forward. If not: adjust the setup and go through Plan again.

↻ …and then the cycle starts again from Plan.

Quick scenario check

SituationYou spot a pattern: three watches in a row from the same batch had the same SIM issue. What's the right first step?
That fixes the symptom (jumping to Do) — but skip Plan and Check, and the same batch problem can keep coming back.
Exactly — that's Plan: understand the issue before acting on the whole batch, not just the three you noticed.

How we classify a defect: AQL

Not every fault is equal. We use an AQL (Acceptable Quality Level) table to decide how strictly a batch is checked, and every defect gets one of three labels:

ClassAcceptance levelExample
Critical0 — none allowedA fault that could stop the SOS alarm from working at all
Major1.0SIM not detected, GPS chip dead, Quick Test Mode doesn't work
Minor2.5Scratched or dirty packaging

Quick check: major or minor?

You open a unit and the GPS chip doesn't respond.
Major. A dead GPS chip is an electronic fault, not cosmetic.
The box has a small scuff mark, but the watch itself works fine.
Minor. Cosmetic packaging issues don't affect function.
The SIM slot doesn't register a SIM at all.
Major. No SIM detection means no connectivity — and no SOS call.
The printed manual insert is missing from the box.
Minor. Doesn't affect the watch's function.

Quick scenario check

SituationA batch of 600 units just arrived from the manufacturer. What applies to the incoming check?
Larger batches get more scrutiny, not less — this is exactly the size where full AQL sampling kicks in.
Exactly — above 500 units, full AQL sampling applies.

Handling Customer Complaints

A complaint isn't paperwork to get through — it's the fastest signal we have that something in the field isn't meeting the requirement. Here's what happens after a partner reports a problem.

📋
Log everything — even if it turns out to be nothing. A pattern only becomes visible if the small reports are recorded too, not just the dramatic ones.
Report comes in

A partner or customer reports an issue with a watch.

At SicuralifeLog it in Odoo's ticketing/assistance module straight away — not just a phone note that might get forgotten.
Monthly triage

Collected reports are reviewed together, not one by one as they trickle in.

At SicuralifeOperations reviews the month's cases and decides: fix internally, or send back to the manufacturer.
Repair or return

Where the fix happens depends on what's actually broken.

🔋
Fixed internally

Battery replacement, refurbish & re-test.

📦
Back to the manufacturer

Screen faults, board-level issues.

Replacement stock

End customers shouldn't be left without a working device while a unit is in repair.

At SicuralifePartners hold a 3% free spare stock for exactly this reason.
Cycle closes

The case is only closed once the loop actually completes.

At SicuralifeA full RMA cycle can take up to ~2 months — not closed the moment a replacement is promised.

Quick scenario check

SituationFive customers this month reported the SOS button doesn't respond. What's the right move?
Five reports of the same safety-critical symptom is exactly the kind of pattern that shouldn't sit until the routine monthly review.
Exactly — a repeated pattern on a safety-critical function like SOS deserves an immediate flag, not the routine cycle.

Your Documented System

Sicuralife's QMS isn't a shelf of binders — it's a small set of living documents in SharePoint. Here's what's where, and the one rule that keeps it useful.

📁
Single source of truth: there's exactly one current version of every document, and it lives in SharePoint — not on your desktop, not in an email attachment, not in a chat message.
DocumentWhat it covers
QM01Quality Management Manual — the big picture
QM02Quality Policy
QM03Organisation chart & responsibilities
QM04Context of the Organisation — stakeholders, risks & opportunities
QF-formsTemplates & records — audits, management review, registers

Do

  • Open the document straight from SharePoint
  • Check you're looking at the current version
  • Ask Lisa or Jérôme if you're not sure which one is current

Don't

  • Save a personal copy on your desktop
  • Keep working from an old emailed attachment
  • Edit a document without it going through review
Who signs off

Lisa reviews, Jérôme approves. If a document doesn't have both, it isn't final yet.

Quick scenario check

SituationAntoine, a new hire, needs the current onboarding checklist. Where should he look first?
A colleague's saved copy might be outdated — that's exactly the local-copy problem we're avoiding.
Exactly — SharePoint is the one place guaranteed to have the current version.

Audits & Management Review

Certification isn't a one-time event — it's the start of a yearly rhythm: internal audits, a management review, and the external audit that leads to (and keeps) the certificate.

TypeWho does itPurpose
Internal audit 1st partySomeone independent of the area being auditedCatch issues before anyone else does
Customer audit 2nd partyA partner or customerConfirms they can rely on us
Certification audit 3rd partyCertification body (SQS)Leads to and maintains the ISO 9001 certificate

Our path to certification

Stage 1

SQS reviews whether the documented system is ready for a full audit.

Close findings

Any gaps from Stage 1 get addressed before Stage 2.

Stage 2

SQS checks that the system is actually followed in practice — not just on paper.

Certificate

Valid for three years, with an annual surveillance audit to keep it.

Five Rules to Remember

  1. Quality means meeting the requirement — every time, not "more luxury."
  2. Plan → Do → Check → Act, then start again. Improvement is a loop, not a one-off project.
  3. Every watch ships with a SIM inserted, and every defect gets classified before it ships.
  4. Log every complaint immediately — patterns only show up if the small ones are recorded too.
  5. One source of truth: SharePoint. No desktop copies, no "final_v3_really_final.docx".
